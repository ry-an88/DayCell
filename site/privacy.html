Privacy

last updated 13 October 2026

The short version

Daycell never receives your journal. There are no accounts. Your entries stay in a file on your device or in your browser’s storage on that device. The only thing that ever leaves your device is, if analytics are switched on, anonymous page counts.

Who we are

Daycell is made by an independent developer in the United Kingdom, who is the data controller for the small amount of data described here. Contact: hello@daycell.app.

Your journal

  • Entries are written to a Markdown file you choose (live files, in Chrome and Edge) or kept in your browser’s local storage on your device.
  • They are never uploaded to us or to anyone else. We cannot read, recover or delete them for you.
  • Browsers can clear stored data, so please keep your own copy (download or eject).

Analytics

If switched on, we use Umami, a cookie-free analytics tool, to count page visits and a few anonymous events (for example “a journal was created”). It does not use cookies, does not track you across sites, and never receives your journal text, titles or tags.

The contact form

If you use the “say hello” form on the home page, your name, email address and message are sent to Netlify Forms, which stores them and emails them to us. We use them only to reply to you, and delete them when you ask. Nothing from your journal is involved.

Hosting

The website is hosted by Netlify, whose servers keep standard access logs (such as IP addresses) for security and reliability. Fonts are served from our own site, not from Google.

Cookies and local storage

Daycell sets no cookies on the website. The app uses your browser’s local storage and IndexedDB only to keep your journal and your settings on your device.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask what personal data we hold about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted. In practice we hold almost nothing beyond emails and form messages you send us. Email hello@daycell.app. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk).

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. The version history is public in the project’s source repository.
