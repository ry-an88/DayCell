the file

open it in anything. it’s yours.

Your journal is a readable Markdown file, not a database. Open it in Notepad, Obsidian or VS Code. Back it up however you like. If Daycell disappeared tomorrow, every word would still be there.

  • One file per journal. Entries are ## headings, oldest first.

faq

questions

Where are my entries saved?

In Chrome and Edge, straight into the .md file you pick on your computer. Everywhere else, in your browser’s storage on that device, until you download or eject the file. Nothing is stored on our side.

Can I lose my journal?

Browsers can clear site data, and Safari does so after 7 days without a visit unless Daycell is on your home screen. That’s why Daycell reminds you to download a copy every few days, and why the file is plain Markdown you can keep anywhere.

Does it sync between devices?

Not through a cloud. Move your .md between devices however you like (download and open it, or keep it in a synced folder with a live file). If you run the free desktop launcher, an iPad on the same Wi-Fi can write into the same file.

Is it really free?

Yes. Every feature is free and there is nothing to buy. Daycell runs in your browser and stores nothing on our side, so it costs very little to keep running.

I use Day One. Can I bring my entries?

Yes, for free. In Day One, export your journal as JSON, then open Daycell and choose “import from Day One” with the .zip. Text, dates, times and tags come across; photos stay in Day One, with a note where each one was.

Can I use it with Obsidian?

Yes. Put the file in your vault. Mood and tags use the same key:: value fields as the Dataview plugin.

Is the code open?

The source is public on GitHub under the Functional Source License: free to read, run and change for anything except a competing product, and every version becomes MIT-licensed two years after release.
